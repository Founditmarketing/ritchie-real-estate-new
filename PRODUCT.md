# Ritchie Real Estate \u2014 Brand Brief

## Register
**Brand.** This is a marketing site where design IS the product. Generic
real-estate-template look-and-feel is the failure mode to avoid.

## Users
- **Buyers** moving to or within Central Louisiana (Alexandria, Pineville,
  Ball, Boyce, Tioga, Forest Hill, Pollock, surrounding parishes). Mix of
  first-time buyers, relocators, and high-end Garden District shoppers.
- **Sellers** who want a real marketing engine, not a sign in the yard.
- **Commercial / investment** clients (the differentiator: CCIM credentials).

## Voice
- Local first. "Cenla" is real shorthand here \u2014 keep it.
- Confident, not slick. "We don't guess the market, we track it."
- The hook is "**Ritchie know.**" Lean into the wordplay; it's the brand.
- Plain English numbers. Don't say "real estate solutions provider."
- Brevity. Editorial typography expects words to earn their space.

## Anti-references
- iHouseweb / generic IDX templates (currently ritchierealestate.com).
- Zillow / Redfin overwhelm \u2014 we are not a search portal.
- "Luxury real estate" gold-foil cliche.
- Stock photo of a smiling family in front of a house.
- Hero-metric template ("12,000 happy clients" with gradient accent).

## Brand identity (from the canonical logo)
The Ritchie mark is a navy-bordered crimson diamond with a serif "R"
monogram, set beside a serif RITCHIE wordmark with a crimson rule. The
visual language is **preppy American heritage**: navy + crimson + cream,
serif-led, confident, established. Not luxury-gold. Not corporate-cool.
Think the cover of a Cenla family\u2019s membership envelope.

## Strategic principles
1. **The broker is the brand.** Matt Ritchie's name, face, and credentials
   are the proof point. Treat the broker page as primary, not boilerplate.
2. **Inventory is the proof.** Listings must feel curated and beautiful even
   when there are only 6 of them, not pretend to be a national portal.
3. **Local matters.** Area guides, market stats, and the Cenla map exist to
   demonstrate, not decorate. Real data, real neighborhoods, real opinion.
4. **Calm density.** A page can carry information without feeling cluttered.
   Editorial grid + serif/sans contrast + earned whitespace.
5. **Motion serves meaning.** Choreography should make navigating feel
   composed, not "watch this animation." If a motion doesn't help the reader
   move through the page, cut it.
